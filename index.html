How to actually learn the neck with this

  1. One chord, five shapes. Play a chord's movable shapes from the nut to the 12th fret without ever jumping more than 4 frets. That ladder is the fretboard map — and the whole-neck view at the top of each chord shows the map the shapes live in.
  2. One string set, three inversions. In Keys, pick a progression and a string set — the trainer shows the smallest-movement path through the inversions. Loop it until the switches are boring.
  3. Recall beats recognition. A few minutes in Drill daily is worth more than an hour of reading diagrams. Missed chords come back around automatically.
  4. Say the notes out loud. Notes mode until you can name every dot, then Intervals mode and name the R–3–5 instead. When both are boring, the neck is yours.

Honest footnotes: impractical grips are omitted or flagged (full G-form barres, some C-form stretches). Spellings are practical guitar names (B♭, E♭, A♭, C♯, F♯ — dim7's double-flat 7 is written as 6). 9th/13th-family grips omit the 5th, as guitarists actually play them — the neck map still shows it. The metronome is scheduled on the audio hardware clock (sub-millisecond jitter) — keep the tab in the foreground on phones.
